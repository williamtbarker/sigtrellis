# Limitations and interpretation contract

This is a tested first research release, not a clinically validated system. Production-oriented packaging and automated checks do not make the methodology universally correct.

1. **Association only.** Neither selection, stability, count-DE support nor strong cross-validation establishes biological validation, a mechanism, causality, clinical utility or qualification. An external assay, independent cohort, and an appropriate intended-use study are separate requirements.
2. **Performance scope.** Nested CV evaluates a training procedure on smaller training sets. It does not automatically evaluate the final full-data fit or the consensus feature panel assembled afterward. A compact assay panel must be frozen and separately assessed. Confidence intervals are not inferred from overlapping folds.
3. **Small studies.** No universal minimum donor count guarantees trustworthy modeling. The default 20-group gate is conservative operational policy, not a power calculation. The public examples deliberately fail the small-cohort gate. Within-group permutations can have very few unique labelings even when many Monte Carlo draws are requested.
4. **Empirical stability.** Overlapping retuned subsamples do not meet all assumptions needed for formal PFER control. Frequencies are not posterior probabilities or p-values. Perturbation-specific frequencies and group stability are descriptive; larger groups mechanically have more chances to be selected.
5. **Confounding.** Perfect batch/phenotype confounding is not computationally resolvable. Purged batch evaluation can become impossible when donors span all batches. Missing batch metadata does not establish robustness. PCA and contingency diagnostics cannot detect every nuisance factor.
6. **Covariates.** Predictive covariates are penalized jointly with genes. No unpenalized logistic nuisance solver or conditional permutation test is provided. When covariates are used, positive robustness gates are withheld because the global permutation test is not a conditional gene test.
7. **DE scope.** Count-aware integration is binary PyDESeq2 with explicit fixed effects, not a general mixed model. Rank-deficient and saturated designs are rejected. Paired fixed effects do not estimate a donor-constant phenotype. Supporting DE is from the same cohort and is not independent validation; p-values after arbitrary upstream selection may not have their nominal interpretation. Multiclass and continuous prediction do not imply support for their count-DE contrasts.
8. **Normalization.** LogCPM and median-ratio transformations assume a meaningful declared gene universe. Relative/compositional shifts can mimic gene changes; strong global RNA-content changes need spike-ins or an appropriate design. Frozen median-ratio fitting can fail in highly sparse training data. TPM/log/integrated matrices cannot be interpreted as raw counts.
9. **Input provenance.** Hidden phenotype encodings, globally selected genes, outcome-assisted embeddings, annotation leakage, externally imputed/scaled matrices, near-duplicate or relabeled specimens, and undisclosed relationships cannot all be detected. Exact target copies and exact duplicated profiles catch specific mistakes, not all leakage. You must supply correct group identities.
10. **Single-cell assumptions.** Pseudobulk targets sample-level expression within supplied cell types. It does not estimate cell abundance, trajectory effects, cellular heterogeneity, or cell-level response probabilities. Doublets, ambient RNA and annotation errors are not automatically solved. Cell-type annotations can already have been influenced by condition or integration. Fixed cell QC still changes the target population. Donor counts remain the sample-size constraint.
11. **Multiple comparisons.** Operational feature gates are not a formal gene-level multiple-testing correction. Binary full-DE tables use backend BH correction within that analysis. The CLI adjusts global permutation eligibility across multiple requested cell types, but post-hoc exploration of many outcomes/configurations still needs independent validation.
12. **Exchangeability.** Group and within-group permutation choices must follow the design. Time trends, treatment-order effects, differential donor sampling and observational covariate structure can invalidate permutation inference. Temporal and censored-outcome analyses are not supported.
13. **Scale and runtime.** Chunked H5AD aggregation avoids a dense cells-by-genes matrix. The sample-level accumulator and models remain dense. Running all cell types can increase memory substantially. Large nested DE/permutation experiments are expensive; no performance promise is made for atlas-scale studies.
14. **Portability.** Seeds, source hashes, versions and thread limits are recorded. Bitwise identity across CPU/BLAS/library versions is not guaranteed. Prepared HDF5/gzip byte streams may vary even when biological counts are identical. A clean Python 3.12 installation is verified; CI definitions for additional Python versions are supplied but are not a report of executed remote CI.
15. **Known deferred methods.** No automatic pathway enrichment, validated gene-program score models, survival analysis, interactions, zero-inflated cell-level models, donor random effects, nonlinear classifiers, nested probability recalibration, train-frozen batch harmonization, or conditional feature-level significance. The current package makes the implemented scope explicit instead of silently approximating these analyses.
